## §1 — Patience, cash, and waiting

> *"The stock market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient."*  
> — **Warren Buffett**

> *"Time is your friend; impulse is your enemy."*  
> — **John Bogle**

> *"The trick is not to learn to trust your gut feelings, but rather to discipline yourself to ignore them."*  
> — **Peter Lynch**

> *"There is always something to do. You just need a higher standard before you act."*  
> — **Charlie Munger** *(attributed)*

> *"Bhav bhagwan che."* (Price is god — respect what the market is telling you.)  
> — **Rakesh Jhunjhunwala** *(Gujarati maxim, widely quoted)*

> *"Cash combined with courage in a crisis is priceless."*  
> — **Warren Buffett**

> *"Opportunities come infrequently. When it rains gold, put out the bucket, not the thimble."*  
> — **Warren Buffett**

## §2 — Don't average losers / sunk cost

> *"Don't average down on a losing position unless the thesis has improved — not because the price fell."*  
> — **Framework synthesis** *(Buffett/Lynch doctrine)*

> *"You don't have to make money back the same way you lost it."*  
> — **Howard Marks**

> *"The most important thing is to avoid big losses. If you avoid those, the gains will take care of themselves."*  
> — **Peter Lynch** *(paraphrase of Lynch loss-avoidance theme)*

> *"Never fall in love with a stock."*  
> — **Peter Lynch**

> *"If you know you're wrong, admit it and get out. Don't try to be both smart and correct."*  
> — **Peter Lynch** *(attributed)*

> *"Cut your losses short and let your winners run."*  
> — **Jesse Livermore** *(classic; use for loss-discipline context only)*

> *"Ek galti ko dusri galti se sudharna risky hota hai."* (Fixing one mistake with another is risky.)  
> — **Indian investing folklore** / **Rakesh Jhunjhunwala** *(widely echoed)*

## §3 — Expensive buying & valuation discipline

> *"Price is what you pay; value is what you get."*  
> — **Warren Buffett**

> *"It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price."*  
> — **Warren Buffett**

> *"The best stock to buy is the one you already own — but only if it's still the best use of your money."*  
> — **Peter Lynch** *(attributed; use to challenge blind averaging)*

> *"Never pay a high price for a stock just because you missed the low."*  
> — **Peter Lynch** *(theme from One Up On Wall Street)*

> *"Growth and value are joined at the hip."*  
> — **Warren Buffett**

> *"Quality never goes out of style, but overpaying for quality does hurt returns."*  
> — **Ramdeo Agrawal** *(QGLP framework theme)*

## §4 — Structural change, moat, and disruption

> *"The investor of today does not profit from yesterday's growth."*  
> — **Warren Buffett**

> *"When a management with a reputation for brilliance tackles a business with a reputation for bad economics, it is the reputation of the business that remains intact."*  
> — **Warren Buffett**

> *"Technology is eating the world — but not every incumbent survives."*  
> — **Marc Andreessen** *(structural-threat context for IT/AI)*

> *"The moat is getting narrower if customers can switch without pain."*  
> — **Framework synthesis** *(Munger/Buffett moat doctrine)*

> *"In a gold rush, sell shovels — or ask who keeps the margin when the rush ends."*  
> — **Analytical proverb**

> *"Regulation and taxation can permanently change economics — not just one quarter's EPS."*  
> — **Framework synthesis** *(India tobacco/GST context)*

## §5 — Cheap for a reason / core-problem test

> *"There is almost always a good reason a stock is cheap."*  
> — **Howard Marks**

> *"A low price alone is not a margin of safety if the business is broken."*  
> — **Framework synthesis** *(Graham/Buffett)*

> *"First find out why the stock is down; then decide if it's temporary or permanent."*  
> — **Peter Lynch** *(One Up On Wall Street theme)*

> *"Risk means more things can happen than will happen."*  
> — **Elroy Dimson**

> *"Bhav gira hai — kya business gira hai ya sirf mood?"* (Price fell — did the business fall or only sentiment?)  
> — **Indian investor heuristic**

---

## §6 — Quality, concentration, and long-term holding

> *"Our favourite holding period is forever."*  
> — **Warren Buffett**

> *"Know what you own, and know why you own it."*  
> — **Peter Lynch**

> *"Wide diversification is only required when investors do not understand what they are doing."*  
> — **Warren Buffett**

> *"Buy right and sit tight."*  
> — **Philip Fisher**

> *"Compounding is the eighth wonder of the world."*  
> — **Albert Einstein** *(attributed; compounding theme)*

> *"Chhote portfolio mein zyada socho, zyada stocks mein kam socho."* (In a small portfolio, think deeply; in many stocks, think shallowly.)  
> — **Vijay Kedia** *(theme: focus on best ideas)*

> *"QGLP — Quality, Growth, Longevity, Price. All four must align."*  
> — **Ramdeo Agrawal**

> — **Rakesh Jhunjhunwala** *(theme)*

> *"Promoter quality and capital allocation matter as much as the sector tailwind."*  
> — **Nemish Shah** *(theme)*

## §8 — When to act vs when to wait (agent default)

> *"Doing nothing is often the right thing to do."*  
> — **Charlie Munger**

> *"You only have to get rich once."*  
> — **Warren Buffett**

> *"The big money is not in the buying or the selling, but in the waiting."*  
> — **Jesse Livermore** *(attributed)*

> *"If you can't hold cash when nothing is attractive, you will own mediocre stocks at mediocre prices."*  
> — **Framework synthesis** *(Marks/Klarman cash discipline)*

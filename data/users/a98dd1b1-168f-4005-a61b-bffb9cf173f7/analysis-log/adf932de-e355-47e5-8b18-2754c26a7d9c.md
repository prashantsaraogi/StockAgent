## Analysis

### 1. Business quality & moat assessment
Historically, Cupid operated as a tender-driven B2B/G2G manufacturer supplying global health organizations (UNFPA, WHO) and government health departments (e.g., South Africa). Institutional tenders provide volume base but carry pricing pressure, tender concentration risk, and volatile working capital cycles. 

The strategy under new leadership is a pivot toward high-margin domestic and international B2C retail brand sales. However, consumer brand equity is currently **unproven** — it is an execution hypothesis rather than a durable moat.

### 2. Management transition & earnings quality
The change of promoter in late 2023 brought aggressive corporate goals and high market visibility. While capital injection and expansion plans are positive intent, new management's track record in FMCG retail distribution remains to be established over a full 3-year operating cycle. Operating cash flow (CFO) to EBITDA/PAT ratio must be monitored closely to ensure reported revenue translates into real cash generation rather than channel inventory buildup.
